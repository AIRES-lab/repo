Project Brief

ERIS: An Interactive Disinformation and Persuasion Simulator

ERIS, the Epistemic Resilience and Influence Simulator, is an experimental AI-based platform for studying and demonstrating how people respond to persuasive manipulation and disinformation in conversational environments.

Rather than presenting participants with static examples of false or misleading content, ERIS places them inside a controlled, fictional scenario and engages them in an adaptive conversation. During the interaction, the system deliberately employs persuasive and manipulative communication strategies while responding to the participant's arguments, doubts, demands for evidence, and attempts at verification. The aim is to reproduce an important feature of contemporary information manipulation: persuasion is increasingly interactive, personalized, and capable of adapting to resistance in real time.

ERIS also examines how participants defend themselves. The interaction can reveal behaviors such as source scrutiny, evidence seeking, counterargumentation, recognition of manipulation, consideration of alternative explanations, and willingness to withhold judgment under uncertainty. It also demonstrates how quickly conversational AI can construct plausible, but potentially incorrect, inferences about a person from a small amount of dialogue.

Each simulation concludes with a research-informed debriefing. Participants are shown how the conversation attempted to influence them, which defensive strategies they employed, what the system inferred from their responses, and where those inferences may be unreliable. The debrief connects the experience to research on misinformation, persuasion, psychological inoculation, epistemic vigilance, and resistance to influence.

ERIS is designed primarily for research, university teaching, and public engagement at the intersection of artificial intelligence, security studies, political communication, and digital literacy. The current prototype uses fictional actors, institutions, events, and claims, allowing participants to experience realistic manipulation without introducing fabricated claims about real political actors or ongoing events.

The broader objective is to move from studying misinformation as a collection of individual messages toward studying dynamic human-AI influence interactions: how persuasive systems learn about their interlocutors, how people adapt in response, and which forms of epistemic resilience remain effective when manipulation itself becomes adaptive. Such systems may also modernize data collection methods in the social sciences.

Why ERIS?

The acronym stands for Epistemic Resilience and Influence Simulator. The name also carries a symbolic meaning: in Greek mythology, Eris is associated with discord and strife.

Project Brief

Tessaron: Exploring Social Responses through AI-Generated Populations

Tessaron is an experimental AI-based research platform for creating and studying synthetic populations: collections of simulated respondents designed to reflect selected characteristics of a population of interest. It explores how generative artificial intelligence can help researchers investigate social attitudes, compare possible responses to information, and develop questions for further empirical study.

The project deploys synthetic personas informed by qualitative and quantitative evidence as a basis for generative agents. In broad terms, the workflow creates synthetic personas, combines them with language models, and uses them to explore how a synthetic panel may respond to structured questions from research instruments such as surveys, interview guides, or codebooks.

A central concern of Tessaron is the distinction between plausible answers and credible research evidence. An AI-generated response can sound convincing without accurately representing human attitudes or behavior. The project therefore places comparison with independent empirical evidence, transparency about uncertainty, and scrutiny of simulated results at the center of its research agenda. Establishing where synthetic samples fail is as important as identifying where they may be useful.

Tessaron is being developed primarily for social science research, with potential applications in security studies, political communication, public opinion research, and research-methods teaching. Its intended uses include exploring hypotheses, pretesting research instruments, and examining alternative scenarios before committing resources to studies with human participants. Synthetic personas are computational cases; their usefulness depends on what can be demonstrated for a particular research purpose.

The broader ambition is to develop a disciplined role for generative simulation in empirical inquiry. By making it possible to explore more scenarios and compare contrasting assumptions, Tessaron aims to help researchers ask sharper questions and identify promising directions for data collection. Its guiding question is how simulated social responses can become a useful, testable complement to the study of real societies.

Core distinction

Tessaron treats synthetic responses as hypotheses and probes, not as substitutes for human evidence. Its value depends on comparison, uncertainty, and clearly stated limits.

e-Infrastructure

Computing support for security research

e-INFRA CZ provides Czech researchers with a common entry point to national research infrastructure: accounts, high-performance and cloud computing, Kubernetes, data storage, repositories, collaboration tools, Jupyter environments, and AI services. For AIRES Lab, this is especially relevant when a seminar paper, thesis, or research project grows beyond a laptop: large datasets, repeated simulations, model inference, reproducible notebooks, or privacy-sensitive computation.

Jan Kleiner, PI of AIRES Lab, also serves as a MetaCentrum ambassador and can help students and colleagues find the right entry point before they move into the official documentation and support channels.

For students

Use e-INFRA as a bridge from classroom methods to real computational research: Jupyter notebooks for analysis, managed environments for R or Python, and shared infrastructure for projects that need more memory, storage, or runtime than a personal computer can provide.

Start with e-INFRA CZ Documentation

For academics

MetaCentrum and related e-INFRA services can support reproducible security studies workflows: batch computing for repeated models, cloud or Kubernetes for services and containers, storage for larger project data, and AI-as-a-Service for secure research-oriented model use.

Open MetaCentrum Documentation

Useful starting points

Begin with account access, then choose the service that matches the research task: Grid for scheduled computation, OnDemand or Jupyter for interactive analysis, storage and repositories for research data, and AI-as-a-Service for secure AI-assisted workflows.
